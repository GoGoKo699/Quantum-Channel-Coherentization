# The cost of a coherent channel interface

A physical channel and a coherent matrix encoding provide different capabilities. The first maps states to states and may discard information into an inaccessible environment. The second acts unitarily on arbitrary vectors of a larger space and can be combined with spectral transformations. Treating the second as a free representation of the first can hide a substantial oracle cost.

For unknown unital channels, the compiler and converse identify that cost's dimension order at fixed precision. Unitality has two separate roles. It makes the Liouville matrix a contraction, so the desired block normalization can be a dimension-independent constant. It also flattens the relevant Choi-program marginal, so the actual collision variance is bounded independently of dimension. The first role was already used in the prior Liouville compiler; retaining the second gives the improved ordinary-query upper bound.

The lower bound has a different mechanism. Even a noiseless unknown unitary channel must be converted into the doubled representation containing both $`U\otimes\overline U`$ and $`U^\dagger\otimes U^{\mathsf T}`$. A successful designated block can therefore expose the unknown inverse. The modern inversion fidelity bound makes the quadratic dimension cost survive a fixed error and arbitrary completion of the block unitary. It does not rely on assuming that the physical compiler can itself be inverted.

Thus one obtains $`\Theta(d^2)`$ ordinary calls at fixed sufficiently small accuracy. For $`n`$ input qubits, $`d=2^n`$, this is $`\Theta(4^n)`$. A smaller known circuit for a structured channel or a stronger supplied interface can change the cost. The theorem is a universal worst-case statement, not a claim that every channel is difficult or that its spectral statistics all require this conversion.

The known-gate implementation and full-channel error are part of the account. Disposable program preparation uses one ordinary channel call, finite rotations require precision allocation, and controlled/inverse target operations are generated with fresh programs. The ideal output block needs only a constant number of ancillary qubits, whereas the physical synthesis needs $`O(\log d)`$ program and work qubits. Query count, gate count, source calibration and hardware runtime remain distinct.

The matching statement is about dimension. The constructive upper has an inverse-error factor, and the block route adds logarithms. The converse gives no matching inverse-error dependence: unknown unitary inversion can be exact with finitely many queries. General noisy unital channels may impose an additional precision obstruction, but establishing its joint law requires separate analysis. The time dependence of the collision construction is likewise not asserted optimal.

Finally, the output is an actual approximate quantum operation. It is not a classical channel estimate, a reconstruction of one expectation value, an exact universal channel adjoint, or a claim of quantum advantage for an application-level task. The [examples](examples.md) show a direct finite-noise recovery capability while preserving those distinctions, and [related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md) identifies the inherited constructions and lower-bound theorems.
